Ski Masks & Balaclavas

A ski mask or balaclava is full coverage: head, face and neck in one piece, built for cold weather and outdoor work. It is a different job from a durag or a bonnet — warmth and protection first, hair second — and it earns its place in the line for anyone who spends real time outside in winter.

Coverage and warmth

A balaclava covers what a hat leaves exposed: ears, neck and the lower face. In wind and cold that coverage is the point. Look for a fit that stays put without fogging glasses or restricting breathing, and for fabric that insulates without trapping sweat on the inside.

Hair and skin underneath

Full coverage traps heat and moisture against hair and skin, so the routine underneath matters: keep hair moisturized before long wear, and let everything breathe and dry when you come inside. Cold-weather beard care follows the same rule — protect it from the wind, then care for it indoors.

Layering with caps

A thin cap underneath a balaclava adds a barrier layer and keeps the balaclava itself cleaner for longer. The same layering logic that protects a wave pattern under a helmet applies here: one layer for the hair, one for the weather.
