Durags

The durag is the foundation of the whole line. It compresses, it protects, and when it fits right it trains the pattern underneath instead of fighting it. This page gathers what durag.store teaches about durags — fabric, fit, tying, washing and styling — so you can pick the right one for your hair and your routine.

Fabric first: silky, velvet, mesh

Silky durags give a smooth, even compression and a clean sheen. Velvet durags add grip and a softer hand, which suits thicker hair and longer wolfing stretches. Mesh durags breathe — the choice for workouts, warm weather and all-day wear. The fabric is not a fashion detail; it decides how the durag sits, how much it slips, and how your pattern sets overnight.

Fit and tying

A durag should sit flat across the forehead without digging in, with the tails long enough to tie securely at the back. Too tight and you get forehead lines and headaches; too loose and it slides off overnight and the pattern goes with it. Tie it the same way every night — consistency is what trains waves, not force.

Washing and making it last

Wash silky and velvet durags gently, by hand or on a delicate cycle, and let them air-dry. Heat and rough handling crush velvet pile and dull silky sheen. Rotate two or three durags instead of wearing one to death, and store them flat or hung so the tails do not tangle.

Durags in the culture

The durag moved from workwear to wave culture to a style icon in its own right. Knowing that history matters when you wear one: it is a tool for hair, and it is also a statement. durag.store treats both sides with respect — the routine and the culture.
