How to Get 360 Waves: The Complete Beginner Roadmap

360 waves are not a product you buy; they are a pattern you train. Your hair already wants to curl — waves are what happens when you brush that curl flat, in the same directions, consistently, and hold it down with compression while it learns. Cross I Bear (@TheCrossIBear) teaches the beginner fundamentals in the series episode below. This roadmap lays out the complete system in the order you will actually live it: the starting cut, the daily brush work, wash days, and the nightly compression that locks it all in.

Start with the right cut

Begin with a low, even cut — most beginners start around a 1.5 to 2 guard, with the grain (WIT), so your barber cuts in the direction your hair grows. Too short and there is no hair to train; too long and you cannot see the pattern forming, which kills motivation in week one. Tell your barber you are starting waves — a good barber will cut with the grain and keep your natural hairline alone.

From that first cut, the rule is simple: brush from the crown outward, in the direction the hair grows, all the way around. Top forward, sides down-forward at an angle, back straight down. That compass never changes, at any level, ever.

The daily brush work

Brush twice a day minimum — a real session in the morning or evening, and shorter touch-ups whenever you pass a mirror. Beginners should aim for one focused session of 15–30 minutes daily. Keep the brush moving in your pattern directions only; random brushing is worse than no brushing because it trains chaos.

Brush on moisturised hair, never bone-dry — dry brushing snaps hair and irritates the scalp. A light moisturiser or a few drops of natural oil worked in first is enough. Then tie your durag straight after the session while the hair is laid: brushing sets the direction, compression memorises it.

Wash days and the first month

Wash once or twice a week using the wash-and-style method — shampoo with the brush working in pattern directions, never scrubbing in circles. Circular scrubbing is the number one pattern-killer for beginners; it undoes a week of brushing in thirty seconds. Condition, brush the product through, then durag it while damp and let it dry under compression.

Expect the first visible ripples in one to three weeks, depending on your hair texture. Coarser hair often shows waves faster; straighter textures take longer and need more wolfing time between cuts. Photograph your crown weekly in the same light — progress you cannot see day to day becomes undeniable in the weekly shots, and that proof is what carries you through the flat days.
