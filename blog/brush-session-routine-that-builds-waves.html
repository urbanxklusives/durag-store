The Brush Session Routine That Actually Builds Waves

Random brushing while watching TV is better than nothing — but a structured session is what builds waves on a schedule. Royalty Headwear (@royaltyheadwear) walks through proper brushing technique in the video below. This guide gives you the full session structure used across the wave community: a warm-up phase, section-by-section work, dedicated crown time, and a finishing phase that sets up your compression. Run it daily and your progress stops being a matter of luck.

Phase 1: Prep and warm-up (3–5 minutes)

Never start on dry, unprepared hair. Work in your moisturiser or a light oil first, distributing it with your hands in pattern directions. Then begin with a medium brush and long, easy strokes around the whole head — this phase is about waking the hair up and spreading product, not force.

Warm-up strokes should feel smooth. If the brush is snagging, your hair is too dry or too tangled from the day — add a touch more moisture and slow down. Snapping through tangles breaks hair and creates the flyaways that make a pattern look fuzzy no matter how well trained it is.

Phase 2: Section work (the bulk of the session)

Now work in your fixed order — top, right side, back, left side — spending equal time on each. Use firmer pressure and deliberate strokes at your correct angles. This is where the training happens: each stroke is a repetition teaching the hair its direction, the same way reps build muscle memory.

A beginner session might give each section three to four minutes (15 minutes total). A serious session doubles that. What matters more than raw minutes is that no section gets skipped — uneven sessions build uneven patterns, and the weak side you ignored becomes next month's troubleshooting project.

Phase 3: Crown focus and the finish

Give the crown its own dedicated minute or two. Brush outward from the exact centre of the swirl in short, careful strokes. The crown is where forks are born and where patterns go to die of neglect — most wavers under-brush it because it is awkward to reach. Awkward is not optional; use a hand mirror or your phone camera to check it.

Finish with a soft brush or a plastic bag / palm pass to lay the surface perfectly flat, then durag immediately. The session is not over when you stop brushing — it is over when the compression is on. Leave laid hair exposed to the air for twenty minutes and you have handed back a chunk of what the session built.
