Waves With Straight or Wavy Hair: The Honest Guide

The question lands in every wave community constantly: can you get waves if your hair is straight or only loosely wavy? The honest answer is yes — with a longer timeline, more length, and more compression than the standard routine. Waves are trained curl laid flat; if your hair has any wave or bend to it at all when it grows, that bend can be trained into a visible pattern. King Infinity's (@KingInfinity) wash method below shows how much definition a wash day can pull out. Here is the adjusted program for straighter textures.

The rule of length

Curl needs length to express itself. Tightly coiled hair shows a wave at half an inch; loosely wavy hair might need two or three inches before the bend becomes a trainable pattern. That means your whole program runs longer: longer growth between cuts, longer wolfing phases, and barbers instructed to cut with length kept on top.

It also means patience through an awkward phase where the hair is long but the pattern is faint. This phase is where most straighter-textured wavers quit — usually about two weeks before the pattern would have shown. Weekly photos are not optional for you; they are the evidence that keeps you in the program.

The adjusted routine

Brush daily in the standard 360 directions, but add sessions: where a coarser texture might train on one session a day, plan on two, and make one of them a mirror session so no angle drifts. Your margin for error is smaller, so your precision has to be bigger.

Compression becomes your best friend. Wear the durag every night without exception, and consider compression during the day at home as well. For straighter textures, time-under-compression does the work that natural curl does for others. The wash method in the video — brush-styling the wash, drying fully under the durag — should be your standing weekly wash, because it manufactures the definition your texture will not give you for free.

Setting expectations honestly

Your finished pattern will likely be wider-spaced and softer-edged than the tight ripples of coarser hair. That is not a failed pattern — it is your texture's version, and a well-maintained wide pattern looks genuinely sharp. Comparing your waves to a different texture's waves is the fastest way to hate a pattern that is actually working.

If after four to six months of honest work — real length, daily sessions, nightly compression — nothing holds, your hair may simply be too straight to lay in a wave, and a textured crop will serve you better. But most people who ask this question have more bend in their hair than they think. Give it the full program before you conclude anything.
