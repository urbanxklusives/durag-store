Kids' Waves: A Gentle Start for Young Kings and Queens

Kids can absolutely run waves — the hair trains the same way — but the routine has to be rebuilt for a child's scalp, patience, and attention span. Softer tools, shorter sessions, gentler products, and above all a tone that makes it a game rather than a chore. Nick Wavy's (@nick_wavy) quick tie tutorial below is the simple tie to teach first, at half speed, together. This guide adapts the beginner wave system for kids, honestly and safely.

The kid-adjusted rules

Soft brushes only. A child's scalp is more sensitive than an adult's, and their hair is often finer — a medium or hard brush that an adult barely feels can scratch and discourage a kid in one session. If the brush is not a pleasure, the routine dies, so the soft brush is not a compromise here; it is the whole strategy.

Sessions run five to ten minutes, max, and end while they are still fun. Two short sessions a day beat one long one for kids. Let them brush in the mirror, let them hold the brush for part of it (with angle guidance), and let the routine belong to them as fast as they can handle it — ownership is what survives into the teenage years.

Products and the cut

Keep products minimal and gentle: a light moisturiser or a drop of natural oil, a gentle shampoo on wash days, nothing heavy, nothing with strong fragrance against young skin. If a product stings, itches, or gets a complaint, believe the complaint and swap it — comfort is a routine feature, not a luxury.

The cut follows the same logic as the adult beginner cut — low and even, with the grain, enough length to show a pattern — and a barber who is good with kids is worth finding and keeping. A kid who likes their barber and their routine will ask for brush time. A kid who dreads both will hide the brush, and honestly, who could blame them.

The durag, kid edition

Teach the tie as a skill to be proud of — the simple standard tie from the video, practiced together a few times, then theirs to do. Tie it gentle: a kid's durag should be snug, never tight, and checked for comfort every time. Soft, stretchy fabrics and shorter tails are easier for small hands to manage.

Some kids will sleep in it proudly; some will have it off by midnight no matter what you do, and the waves will still come — slower, with more brush time making up the difference. Protect the fun first and the pattern second. A child who grows up loving the culture of caring for their crown keeps these habits for life; the waves are just the first trophy.
