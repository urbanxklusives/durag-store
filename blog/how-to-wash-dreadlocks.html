How to Wash Dreadlocks: Clean Locs Without the Residue

Washing locs is not harder than washing loose hair — it is different, in three specific ways: shampoo has to reach a scalp hidden under a mass of hair, product has to be worked through dense lengths instead of over them, and the locs must dry completely or pay for it later. The wash routine in the video below, from Navigate Life With Tansyong (@JonathanOrbuda), shows the real process on real locs. This guide covers each phase and the residue problem that defines loc washing.

Getting to the scalp

Wet the locs thoroughly first — fully saturated locs accept shampoo; surface-wet locs repel it. Apply shampoo to the scalp in sections, working it in with your fingertips between the locs, and let the suds travel down the lengths as you squeeze the locs like sponges. That squeezing action is the loc wash's core move: it pumps cleanser through the loc instead of letting it skate over the surface.

Two washes are normal for locs — the first lifts the surface dirt and oil, the second actually cleans. If your wash water and suds run grey on the first pass, that is information, not embarrassment: locs hold onto more than loose hair does, which is the whole point of the second pass.

The residue problem

Residue is the loc wearer's long-term enemy: product, minerals, and lint trapped inside the loc, building up invisibly until locs feel heavy, look dull, and hold a smell no wash seems to fix. Prevention beats cure. Choose shampoos that rinse clean — heavy conditioning shampoos and waxy products are the usual sources — and rinse far longer than feels necessary, squeezing sections under the running water until it runs perfectly clear.

Conditioner, if you use it, goes lightly on the lengths and rinses thoroughly; many loc wearers skip it or use diluted sprays instead. Whatever goes in must be able to come out — that single rule, applied to every product decision, prevents most residue stories.

Drying: all the way through

A loc that looks dry can be wet at its core for hours, and a loc that stays damp at the core regularly will eventually smell like it. After washing, squeeze the lengths thoroughly with a towel, then air-dry with the locs loose and moving — a fan speeds it — or sit under a dryer until the thickest locs are dry when squeezed hard.

Never tie locs up, cover them, or sleep on them while they are still damp inside. Wash day scheduling matters: wash with enough hours ahead of bed for a full dry. It is the least glamorous rule in loc care and one of the most important — clean locs, fully dried, are locs that stay fresh for years.
