How to Wear a Bonnet Correctly: Fit, Band Placement, and Hair Prep

A bonnet looks foolproof — it is a bag for your hair, how wrong can it go? Wrong enough that most bonnet disappointment traces back to wearing, not the bonnet: hair stuffed instead of arranged, the band sawing across the edges, a size chosen for the head instead of the hair. MadamSew's (@MadamSew) construction video below shows what a bonnet is built to do. This guide covers the wearing: prep, placement, and the checks that make a bonnet protective instead of decorative.

Prep: arrange the hair first

A bonnet preserves the arrangement it is given. Hair thrown in tangled wakes up tangled, just warmer. Before the bonnet goes on: detangle gently, arrange the style the way you want it to rest — braids draped, curls gathered up in a loose pineapple, locs swept back — and add moisture if the hair is dry, because the bonnet will hold that moisture in beautifully.

Loose styles benefit from a quick gathering: a loose scrunchie (satin or smooth) at the top keeps long hair from pooling at the nape, where it rubs and mats. Thirty seconds of prep is the difference between a bonnet and a bag.

Placement: band position is everything

Open the bonnet wide, place it over the arranged hair from front to back, and seat the band so it runs behind the ears and across the lower back of the head. The band should sit at the hairline's edge — never across the forehead skin in a way that drags, and never sawing directly on fragile front edges under tension.

Tuck any escapees in with your fingertips, then do the shake test: nod twice, turn side to side. A correctly seated bonnet with a healthy band does not migrate. If it shifts during the shake test, it will be gone by 3 a.m. — reseat it lower at the back or address the band before sleep, not after.

Sizing for hair, not head size

Bonnet sizes describe the pocket, and the pocket must fit your hair's volume: braids, twists, and long locs need jumbo capacity regardless of your head's circumference. A pocket stuffed to its limit pushes the band off its seat all night — the slow-motion version of the bonnet on the pillow.

Keep two signals in mind when judging yours: morning edges intact and uncreased (good), a band mark pressed deep into the forehead or hair escaped and matted at the nape (bad). The bonnet is the simplest tool in this entire guide — worn right, it disappears, and that disappearance is exactly what correct feels like.
